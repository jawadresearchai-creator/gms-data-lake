# Corporate Science Entry v1 — THEORY REFRAMING LOCK

## Status
This document freezes the post-analysis manuscript framing after the empirical design, H1–H5 definitions, primary estimates, inference hardening, sensitivity grid, and manuscript claim boundary were already locked.

**This is a manuscript-framing change, not an analytical change.** No hypothesis, treatment definition, outcome, sample rule, estimator, robustness test, or sensitivity design may be altered to improve the narrative.

## Working title
**The Limits of First-Mover Advantage in Corporate Science: Entry Timing, Persistence, and International Knowledge Networks**

Alternative acceptable title:
**When Being Early Is Not Enough: Corporate Scientific Entry, Persistence, and International Knowledge Networks in Emerging Fields**

The title may later be shortened for a target journal, but it must not imply a robust positive H5 effect or a causal first-mover benefit.

## Original research question retained
**Do firms that enter newly emerging scientific domains earlier develop a persistent scientific advantage, and does the diversity of their external international research networks condition that advantage?**

## Hypotheses remain frozen
- **H1:** Earlier relative corporate entry is associated with higher subsequent firm-topic scientific output.
- **H2:** Earlier relative corporate entry is associated with greater persistence of firm activity in the entered scientific topic.
- **H3:** Earlier relative corporate entry is associated with higher field-year-normalized citation impact after entry.
- **H4:** Greater pre-entry international collaboration-network diversity is associated with earlier relative entry into emerging scientific topics.
- **H5:** Pre-entry international collaboration-network diversity positively moderates the association between earlier entry and subsequent persistence.

No hypothesis may be rewritten to match the realized sign.

# Locked empirical message

1. **No general output advantage from earlier entry.** H1 is not supported.
2. **No general persistence advantage from earlier entry.** H2 is not supported; estimates are predominantly negative, including every +5 sensitivity estimate.
3. **Citation-impact advantage is unresolved in the primary design.** H3 is not estimable under the frozen support rule; sensitivity-only estimates are near zero and non-significant.
4. **Network diversity does not select firms into earlier entry.** H4 is not supported, and all 13 gate-passing sensitivity coefficients are negative.
5. **The only recurring positive pattern is conditional.** H5 is positive in most frozen definitions and all leave-one-firm-out refits, but small-cluster inference is sensitive. It is qualified directional evidence only.

# Reframed theoretical contribution

## Contribution 1 — Boundary condition on first-mover advantage
The paper's first contribution is **not** that corporate scientific first movers outperform later entrants. The contribution is that the conventional expectation of an automatic first-mover advantage does **not travel cleanly into corporate scientific domains**.

Corporate entry into an emerging scientific topic differs from market entry. Scientific entry occurs under substantial technological and epistemic uncertainty, offers weak or delayed appropriability, requires continuing allocations to researchers and complementary capabilities, and may expose firms to knowledge spillovers before a stable commercial opportunity is visible. Early positioning therefore creates an opportunity to learn, but it does not itself imply durable scientific output or persistence.

The empirical boundary condition is strong because both H1 and H2 fail under the frozen primary design and across the prespecified sensitivity grid. The manuscript should therefore challenge a simple `earlier → advantage` model rather than apologize for failed hypotheses.

## Contribution 2 — Separate entry timing from the ability to sustain entry
The second contribution is conceptual separation between **arriving early** and **remaining capable of participating**.

The results imply that the strategic problem is not merely `when to enter?` but also `what capabilities make early entry sustainable?`

The predominantly negative H2 direction is especially useful as a boundary condition: early entry may expose firms to exploration costs, immature knowledge, unstable trajectories, or premature commitment. Those mechanisms are **not directly tested in the present data** and therefore must appear only as theoretically motivated explanations/future tests, not as findings.

Permitted language:
- "The predominantly negative persistence estimates are consistent with the possibility that early scientific entry entails exploration and commitment costs."

Prohibited language:
- "Early entry creates exploration costs."
- "Early entry reduces persistence because firms commit prematurely."

## Contribution 3 — Capability-contingent rather than timing-deterministic advantage
The H4/H5 combination creates the paper's most interesting contingency result.

- H4 shows that internationally diverse scientific networks do **not** predict which firms enter earlier.
- H5 shows a recurring positive interaction between earlier entry and pre-entry network diversity for persistence, although inference is sensitive.

This supports a **conditioning rather than selection** interpretation:

`network diversity ≠ earlier arrival`

but potentially:

`earlier arrival × diverse knowledge access → more favorable persistence`

The manuscript may interpret this through absorptive-capacity, knowledge-based, and network-capability lenses. It must not claim that the mechanism has been directly identified.

## Contribution 4 — Specification-stability as substantive evidence
The manuscript should treat the frozen 13-design sensitivity grid as part of the scientific contribution rather than a defensive appendix.

The important result is that **sign stability and significance stability diverge**:
- H5 +3 is positive in 12/13 balanced gate-passing definitions.
- Yet CRV3 and firm wild-bootstrap inference do not support a robust-significance claim.

That distinction demonstrates why a single preferred p-value is an inadequate summary. The contribution is methodological discipline: reasonable measurement and inference choices are incorporated into the substantive interpretation.

# Theory architecture for the paper

## Theoretical tension
The Introduction should establish a tension between two mechanisms.

### Early-entry opportunity mechanism
Earlier entry may provide:
- more time for cumulative learning;
- position in developing knowledge networks;
- earlier recognition of scientific opportunities;
- the ability to shape internal routines around a developing domain;
- potential first access to scarce scientific partners/talent.

### Early-entry liability mechanism
Earlier entry may simultaneously impose:
- greater scientific and technological uncertainty;
- higher likelihood of pursuing dead-end trajectories;
- immature complementary assets and partner ecosystems;
- costly experimentation before field standards stabilize;
- premature organizational commitment;
- larger knowledge-integration burden.

The ex ante hypotheses remain directional toward first-mover advantage, but the Discussion should interpret the realized null/negative pattern as evidence that the liability side of the theoretical trade-off cannot be ignored in corporate science.

# Core manuscript thesis

**Corporate scientific first-mover advantage is not automatic. Earlier entry into emerging scientific domains does not, by itself, predict greater subsequent output or persistence. The most persistent positive pattern is conditional: firms entering with more diverse international scientific knowledge networks exhibit a more favorable early-entry–persistence association, although this moderation remains inference-sensitive.**

# Contribution paragraph blueprint

The Introduction should end with four claims, calibrated as follows:

1. **First**, the study extends entry-timing research upstream from product/technology markets into corporate participation in emerging scientific knowledge domains and identifies a boundary condition to the assumption that earlier entry generates durable advantage.
2. **Second**, it distinguishes the timing of entry from the persistence of scientific participation, showing that early arrival alone is not associated with sustained firm-topic activity.
3. **Third**, it provides qualified evidence that pre-entry international knowledge-network diversity may condition the persistence implications of early entry without itself predicting earlier entry.
4. **Fourth**, it demonstrates how frozen alternative definitions and small-cluster inference can materially alter the interpretation of apparent first-mover effects, separating coefficient-direction stability from statistical-significance stability.

# Literature anchors

The revised paper should be grounded in the following literature families:

## First-mover / entry timing
- Classical first-mover advantage and entry-order theory.
- Cirik & Makadok: distinction between observed first-mover advantage and causal first-mover benefit; endogenous entry timing.
- Recent entry-timing work emphasizing preemption-versus-learning trade-offs and uncertainty rather than universal early-entry benefits.
- Emerging-technology studies showing nonlinear or contingent early-mover performance.

## Corporate science and absorptive capacity
- Cohen & Levinthal: absorptive capacity and prior knowledge.
- Fabrizio: internal research + university collaboration improve external search timing/quality.
- Corporate scientific publication and science–technology linkage research showing value depends on the ability to connect science to downstream technological use.

## Networks and knowledge integration
- Collaboration-network work showing network position/diversity effects are contingent on absorptive or recombinative capabilities.
- Recent work treating heterogeneous external knowledge as potentially valuable but integration-intensive.

# Reviewer attacks and locked responses

## Attack: "Most hypotheses failed, so the paper has no contribution."
Response: The contribution is a theoretically informative boundary condition. A rigorously estimated absence of the expected main effect across a frozen sensitivity grid rejects the idea that first-mover logic automatically applies to corporate science. The conditional H5 pattern offers a capability-based explanation worthy of further testing without being overstated.

## Attack: "This is just a null-results paper."
Response: It is not a collection of isolated nulls. H1/H2/H4 form a coherent pattern against timing-deterministic advantage; H5 reveals a recurring conditional direction; H2 is predominantly negative across horizons; H3's non-estimability is preserved transparently. The contribution is the configuration of results and the boundary condition it defines.

## Attack: "H5 is fragile."
Response: Yes. The paper explicitly reports that coefficient direction is stable but inferential significance is not. H5 is theory-building directional evidence, not the sole basis of the paper and not a confirmed hypothesis.

## Attack: "You are inventing a story after seeing results."
Response: H1–H5, treatment definitions, outcomes, primary design, sensitivity grid, and claim boundary remain frozen. The reframing changes only how the realized evidence is positioned relative to theory. No analytical choice is altered to create the revised story.

## Attack: "Negative H2 proves first-mover disadvantage."
Response: No. The primary H2 estimate is not conventionally significant under the main inference methods, although the direction is predominantly negative across prespecified sensitivities. The manuscript may discuss early-entry liabilities as a plausible mechanism but cannot claim a demonstrated first-mover disadvantage.

The paper must be written as:

**a boundary-condition and contingency study of scientific entry timing**

and not as:

- a successful first-mover-advantage confirmation;
- a causal effect of early entry;
- a paper rescued by H5;
- a significance-hunting exercise;
- a claim that earlier entry is harmful.

The empirical integrity of the paper depends on keeping the failed directional hypotheses visible and making the revised contribution about what those failures reveal.
