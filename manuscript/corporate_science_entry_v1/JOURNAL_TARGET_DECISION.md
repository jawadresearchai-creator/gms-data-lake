# Corporate Science Entry v1 — Journal Target Decision

## Decision status

**PRIMARY TARGET: TECHNOVATION**  
**STRETCH TARGET: RESEARCH POLICY**  
**STRONG FALLBACK: R&D MANAGEMENT**

This journal strategy is based on the frozen empirical evidence, the hostile-reviewer audit, and current official journal scopes checked on 8 September 2026. It does not change the analysis, hypotheses, or locked scientific claims.

## 1. Primary target — Technovation

### Why it is the best current fit

Technovation positions itself as an interdisciplinary journal of technological innovation, entrepreneurship, and technology management. Its scope explicitly includes technological trends and breakthroughs, management of technological innovation in medium-sized and large organizations, organizational structures and practices, investment strategies involving new science- or technology-based enterprise, and technology-intensive managerial activity.

The current paper fits this scope through its focus on:

- corporate participation in emerging scientific domains;
- strategic timing of observable scientific entry;
- persistence of corporate research activity;
- international scientific collaboration networks;
- organizational capabilities and absorptive-capacity arguments; and
- implications for management of science-intensive innovation.

### Why Technovation is preferable to Research Policy for v1

The paper has unusually transparent design and novel cross-domain data, but its strongest limitations are difficult to remove without a new data expansion: only 21 firms in the primary panel, a sample-relative rather than population-wide corporate frontier, endogenous entry timing, publication-based entry measurement, and inference-sensitive H5 evidence. Technovation offers a high-quality innovation-management venue where a theory-building boundary-condition result can be central without requiring the paper to establish a population-wide or causal first-mover effect.

### Required positioning for Technovation

The paper should emphasize:

> **Early entry into observable corporate science does not generate an automatic persistence or output premium; the more plausible strategic question is how firms' external knowledge architecture conditions whether early scientific participation can be sustained.**

The paper should not be sold as a bibliometric study. OpenAlex data are an empirical measurement infrastructure for an organizational and technology-management question.

## 2. Stretch target — Research Policy

### Scope fit

Research Policy is an exceptionally strong substantive fit. Its stated scope includes management and organizational challenges in science, technology, R&D and innovation; knowledge creation, acquisition and diffusion; organizational learning; research/R&D collaboration networks; R&D management and strategy; and firm capabilities including absorptive capabilities.

Thus, the topic itself belongs comfortably within Research Policy.

### Why it is a stretch rather than the recommended first submission

The present version faces several reviewer risks that matter more at a journal where readers may expect very strong generalizability or identification:

1. the primary panel contains only 21 firms;
2. the corporate adoption frontier is relative to linked firms, not the verified first corporation in the population;
3. entry timing remains endogenous despite weighting and fixed effects;
4. H1, H2, and H4 are unsupported;
5. H3 is not estimable in the primary design; and
6. H5 is directionally stable but not robust to the most conservative small-cluster inference.

A Research Policy submission would therefore need to lead with a particularly sharp theoretical boundary condition and methodological transparency rather than with a strong positive treatment effect. It would be a reasonable prestige-first gamble, but not the best acceptance-probability decision for the current v1 dataset.

### What would materially improve Research Policy readiness

A later v2 could become substantially stronger if it adds one or more of:

- broader firm coverage and additional validated SEC↔OpenAlex links;
- a more population-complete corporate frontier;
- an exogenous or quasi-exogenous entry-timing source;
- richer measures of partner/network quality and internal absorptive capacity;
- patent or commercialization outcomes linked to scientific entry; or
- replication of the H5 direction in a larger corporate sample.

## 3. Strong fallback — R&D Management

### Scope fit

R&D Management explicitly covers research, development, design and innovation management from exploratory science through commercial exploitation, with implications for managers, R&D teams, and organizations. This makes the corporate-science timing question a direct scope match.

### Strategic advantages

- The exploratory-science context is central rather than peripheral to the journal's remit.
- The study has clear implications for R&D management: being early is not itself a sufficient strategy, and external scientific knowledge access may shape persistence.
- Transparent null/boundary-condition findings can be framed as a managerial correction to simplistic first-mover prescriptions.

### Submission constraint

Current Wiley author instructions state that Research Papers have a maximum main-body length of **8,000 words**, excluding references, tables, figures, and appendices, with a **150–250 word abstract**. Therefore, the current complete manuscript must be compressed materially before an R&D Management submission.

## 5. Frozen submission sequence

Unless the empirical dataset is materially expanded before submission, the recommended sequence is:

1. **Technovation** — first submission.
2. **R&D Management** — second submission if Technovation rejects after editorial or peer review.
3. **Research Policy** should be treated as an optional prestige-first substitution for step 1 only if the author explicitly prefers higher rejection risk over the current fit/acceptance strategy.

This sequence should not be changed because a journal appears to favor statistically significant results. Journal choice is based on substantive fit with the actual locked evidence.

## 6. Technovation-specific manuscript transformation plan

Before submission, create a journal-specific derivative of the frozen complete manuscript while keeping the journal-neutral master unchanged.

### Title

Recommended:

**When Early Entry Is Not Enough: Corporate Scientific Entry, Persistence, and International Knowledge Networks**

### Abstract architecture

1. Strategic problem: firms enter emerging scientific domains under uncertainty.
2. Gap: evidence on first-mover advantage does not establish whether earlier corporate scientific production creates persistent advantage.
3. Data/design: firm-topic panel, objective topic emergence, relative entry, international pre-entry networks, frozen multiverse/sensitivity framework.
4. Main result: no general output/persistence premium; no network-diversity antecedent of earlier entry.
5. Conditional result: early-entry–persistence association becomes more favorable at higher network diversity, but inference is sensitive.
6. Contribution: contingency/boundary condition on scientific first-mover logic.

### Theory emphasis

Lead with the conflict between:

- cumulative learning / preemption from earlier participation; and
- uncertainty / premature commitment / follower learning.

Use absorptive capacity and international knowledge networks to motivate the conditional mechanism without claiming that network entropy directly measures absorptive capacity.

### Results emphasis

Main text should contain:

- sample and balance table;
- one locked hypothesis-results table with CRV1, CRV3, two-way, and wild-bootstrap inference;
- a conceptual entry-timing figure showing global emergence, linked corporate frontier, early window, and later entrant;
- H5 frozen sensitivity visualization; and
- H5 leave-one-firm-out visualization.

Do not give equal prose space to every sensitivity coefficient.

### Discussion emphasis

The central contribution is not “H5 is significant.” It is:

> **The empirical boundary of first-mover advantage appears earlier than commonly assumed: early observable corporate scientific entry alone does not create a general scientific performance premium, while the recurring conditional network pattern suggests that external knowledge architecture may determine whether early participation becomes sustainable.**

## 7. Journal-decision lock

The following are prohibited journal-positioning strategies:

- changing the scientific hypothesis to fit a target journal;
- presenting the paper as causal to appear stronger;
- selecting only H5-friendly inference procedures;
- calling sample-relative early entrants global first movers;
- hiding unsupported H1/H2/H4 or non-estimable H3;
- choosing a journal because it appears more receptive to p<0.05 findings.

The journal-specific paper must remain a derivative of the frozen analysis and manuscript claim locks.
